# Showcase collection: authors of African descent

Candidate books for the public showcase library (`docs/PLAN.md`, Phase 2). Built 2026-11-24.
Copyright rule: **US public domain** ([DR-0011](../decisions/DR-0011-showcase-copyright-rule.md)).

- **240 authors searched.** 187 have at least one digital copy, and 53 have none found yet.
- **2,085 cleared books, which can go straight in.** Only books where the person is the author count. Books where they were the editor, a contributor, the translator or the introduction writer are left out.
  - 62 on Standard Ebooks and 414 on Project Gutenberg. Both sites check US copyright before publishing.
  - Alexandre Dumas (père) alone accounts for 167 of the Gutenberg books: French originals, several translations and multi-volume splits of the same novels. Choose one edition per novel.
  - 37 from DocSouth: hand-corrected transcriptions, published by 1930, under CC BY 4.0.
  - 1,572 from HathiTrust, where its own copyright review opened them as public domain (`pd`
    everywhere, or `pdus` for the US only). These are page scans, so each needs OCR text or an
    EPUB made from it. Dumas, Pushkin and Booker T. Washington account for most of them.
- **1,097 leads to check before adding:**
  - 866 archive.org scans dated 1930 or earlier whose creator matches the author's name. Check the author, the first-edition date and the scan quality.
  - 16 HathiTrust titles that look like sheet music.
  - 215 DocSouth texts by authors not on the seed list. These are mostly first-person slave narratives and Black church histories. Check each author (see [DocSouth](#docsouth)).
- **Every book, with links:** [`african-descent-candidates.csv`](african-descent-candidates.csv). Sort by `source`. Best editions first: Standard Ebooks, then DocSouth or Gutenberg, then HathiTrust and archive.org scans. The per-author tables below count Standard Ebooks, Gutenberg and archive.org only; the [report page](https://claude.ai/artifact/EkrNcRMEaEtkbdfGJPLJP1) counts every source.

## How this list was built

1. **Authors:** Wikidata (people of African descent with a Gutenberg ID or a Wikisource page),
   Gutenberg's "African American Writers" shelf, and a hand-made list covering the US, the
   Caribbean, Britain, Europe, Latin America and Africa ([seeds](african-descent-seeds.tsv)).
   The hand-made list is from memory (ASSUMPTION: each person's African descent, as commonly
   documented in their biographies).
2. **Standard Ebooks:** each author page was fetched.
3. **Gutenberg:** the full catalogue (`pg_catalog.csv`, from the gutenberg.pglaf.org mirror,
   because gutenberg.org was down for maintenance), matched by author name. Six same-name
   matches were removed by hand.
4. **archive.org:** an advanced search per author, kept only when the full name matches and, if
   both records give one, the birth year matches. Letters and manuscripts were dropped.
5. **HathiTrust:** the full Hathifile (`hathi_full_20261101.txt.gz`, 1.2 GB, from
   [Hathifiles](https://www.hathitrust.org/member-libraries/resources-for-librarians/data-resources/hathifiles/)),
   matched with `scripts/hathi.py`. Only `pd` and `pdus` records are kept.
   - A record has to match the author's known birth or death year. If neither year is known, it
     has to match a full three-part name.
   - Dropped: books the author only edited; John Brown's records (they belong to the Scottish
     doctor of that name); and the authors who came only from DocSouth, because there are no
     dates to tell them apart from others with the same name.
6. **Death years:** from Wikidata where available. DR-0011 uses them only if a takedown request
   arrives.

**Gaps:**
- Authors writing in French, Portuguese, Spanish and African languages are under-covered,
  because their books sit in national libraries such as
  [Gallica](https://gallica.bnf.fr) and the [Biblioteca Nacional Digital](https://bndigital.bn.gov.br).
- Several books were dictated to, or written down by, a white editor: Charles Ball, Mary Prince, Nat Turner, Old Elizabeth, Sojourner Truth, Venture Smith.
  Credit both people in the metadata.
- **Coming on 1 January 2027:** books first published in 1931 enter the US public domain.

Link key: SE = Standard Ebooks, PG = Gutenberg search, IA = archive.org search,
Hathi = HathiTrust (full view only), WS = Wikisource.

## DocSouth

[Documenting the American South](https://docsouth.unc.edu/) (UNC Chapel Hill) was archived on
30 March 2026, and the live site now refuses connections. Its bulk "DocSouth Data" downloads
survive in the Wayback Machine, checked 2026-11-25 and confirmed intact with `unzip -t`:

| Collection | Wayback copy |
|---|---|
| North American Slave Narratives | [na-slave-narratives.zip](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916203045/https://docsouth.unc.edu/full-text/na-slave-narratives.zip) (49 MB) |
| First-Person Narratives of the American South | [first-person-narratives-american-south.zip](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916203138/https://docsouth.unc.edu/full-text/first-person-narratives-american-south.zip) (36 MB) |
| The Church in the Southern Black Community | [church-southern-black-community.zip](https://web.archive.org/web/20261102190047/https://docsouth.unc.edu/full-text/church-southern-black-community.zip) (30 MB) |
| Library of Southern Literature | [library-southern-literature.zip](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916203139/https://docsouth.unc.edu/full-text/library-southern-literature.zip) (39 MB) |

- **Contents:** each ZIP holds plain text and TEI/XML for every title, plus a `toc.csv` (title, author, date, URL).
- **Licence:** the readme says the texts, encoding and metadata are under
  [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The one exception is *Fields's
  Observation*, which has no known rights. Credit "University Library, The University of North
  Carolina at Chapel Hill" on every DocSouth-sourced book.
- **Sorting:** all 711 titles were matched against the seed list.
  - 37 new titles for seed authors are marked cleared.
  - 215 texts by other authors from the Slave Narratives and Church collections, or with a race
    keyword in the title, are kept as leads.
  - 80 texts by authors I know to be white (abolitionist biographers, pro-slavery writers,
    Mark Twain, and so on) were dropped. This list is from memory (ASSUMPTION).
  - The Library of Southern Literature and First-Person Narratives collections are otherwise
    mostly white Southern authors, so those were skipped.
- **Ebooks:** none are included. The TEI/XML would need converting to EPUB, which is work for
  the ingest step.
